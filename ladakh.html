WELCOME TO LADAKH

About Ladakh

Ladakh is a Union Territory in the northern part of India, known for its high mountain ranges, cold desert landscapes, valleys and traditional Buddhist monasteries.

Leh is the principal town and administrative centre. The region includes the districts of Leh and Kargil and is known for its unique geography and cultural diversity.

HISTORY

A Heritage of Himalayan Civilisation

Ladakh has a long history as a meeting point of cultural and trading routes across the Himalayas and Central Asia. Its monasteries, palaces and traditional settlements preserve important parts of this history.

Under the Jammu and Kashmir Reorganisation Act, 2019, Ladakh became a separate Union Territory on 31 October 2019.

PLACES TO EXPLORE

Famous Tourist Destinations

Pangong Tso

A spectacular high-altitude lake famous for its changing shades of blue and mountain surroundings.

Nubra Valley

A beautiful valley known for dramatic landscapes, sand dunes and traditional villages.

Thiksey Monastery

A prominent Buddhist monastery with traditional architecture and views of the Indus Valley.

Hemis Monastery

One of Ladakh's important monasteries, known for its religious heritage and annual festival.

Shanti Stupa

A white-domed Buddhist monument overlooking Leh and the surrounding mountains.

Leh Palace

A historic royal palace offering a glimpse into Ladakh's former royal life and architecture.

Magnetic Hill

A well-known roadside attraction near Leh, popular for its unusual visual perspective.

Kargil

A historic town in the Suru Valley region, surrounded by impressive mountain scenery.

CULTURE & TRADITIONS

Life in the Mountains

Ladakh has a diverse cultural identity shaped by Buddhist, Muslim and other local communities. Monasteries, traditional festivals, folk music, dance and local crafts are important parts of its heritage.

Traditional architecture often uses locally available materials and is adapted to the region's cold and dry climate.

FOOD & FLAVOURS

Traditional Ladakhi Food

Ladakhi cuisine includes dishes such as thukpa, skyu, momos, tingmo and butter tea. Barley is an important traditional crop, and roasted barley flour called tsampa is used in local food.

NATURE & WILDLIFE

A Unique High-Altitude Ecosystem

Ladakh's cold desert environment supports specialised wildlife. The snow leopard, Himalayan ibex, blue sheep and black-necked crane are among the region's notable animals and birds.

Hemis National Park is particularly important for snow leopard conservation and high-altitude biodiversity.
