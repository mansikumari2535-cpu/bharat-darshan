Welcome to Rajasthan

Rajasthan is the largest state in India by geographical area. Located in the northwestern part of the country, it is famous for its royal history, magnificent architecture, desert landscapes, traditional crafts and vibrant festivals.

Jaipur, the state capital, is popularly known as the Pink City. Rajasthan shares an international border with Pakistan and is home to the Thar Desert, the Aravalli Range and many historic cities.

History of Rajasthan

Rajasthan has a long and remarkable history shaped by ancient settlements, Rajput kingdoms and powerful rulers. Its historic cities and forts reflect centuries of political, cultural and architectural development.

The region was historically associated with several kingdoms, including Mewar, Marwar, Amber and Bikaner. The forts of Chittorgarh, Kumbhalgarh and Jaisalmer preserve important parts of this history.

The modern state of Rajasthan emerged through the integration of princely states after India's independence.

Famous Tourist Destinations

Jaipur

Known for Hawa Mahal, Amber Fort, City Palace and its historic pink-coloured architecture.

Udaipur

Famous for Lake Pichola, City Palace and scenic lakes surrounded by hills.

Jaisalmer

Known for its golden sandstone fort, historic havelis and desert landscapes.

Jodhpur

The Blue City, famous for Mehrangarh Fort and traditional old-town streets.

Mount Abu

A hill station known for Dilwara Temples, Nakki Lake and green surroundings.

Pushkar

A historic town known for Pushkar Lake, its temples and annual camel fair.

Culture and Traditions

Rajasthan is celebrated for its colourful clothing, folk music, traditional dance forms, handicrafts and warm hospitality. Ghoomar and Kalbelia are among its well-known dance traditions.

Traditional arts include miniature paintings, blue pottery, block printing, embroidery, leatherwork and jewellery making. Turbans and colourful textiles are also important parts of its cultural identity.

Famous Food of Rajasthan

Dal Baati Churma

A famous combination of lentils, baked wheat balls and sweet churma.

Gatte ki Sabzi

A traditional curry made with gram-flour dumplings.

Ker Sangri

A distinctive desert-region dish prepared with local beans and berries.

Laal Maas

A traditional spicy meat curry associated with Rajasthani cuisine.

Festivals of Rajasthan

Rajasthan celebrates many colourful festivals throughout the year. The Pushkar Camel Fair, Desert Festival of Jaisalmer, Teej, Gangaur and Mewar Festival showcase the state's traditions, music, crafts and community life.

Wildlife and Natural Heritage

Rajasthan has diverse natural landscapes, from desert ecosystems to forests and wetlands. Ranthambore National Park is known for its tiger habitat, while Keoladeo National Park is an important bird sanctuary and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

The Desert National Park protects parts of the Thar Desert ecosystem and is an important habitat for the Great Indian Bustard.
