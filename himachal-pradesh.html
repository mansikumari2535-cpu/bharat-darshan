WELCOME TO HIMACHAL PRADESH

The Land of Snow-Capped Mountains

Himachal Pradesh is a beautiful Himalayan state in northern India. Established as a full state on 25 January 1971, it is celebrated for its mountain scenery, forests, rivers, traditional villages and distinctive regional cultures.

From the colonial-era streets of Shimla to the dramatic high-altitude landscapes of Spiti, every region has its own character. The state is also known for its wooden temples, colourful festivals, handicrafts and warm local traditions.

Famous Places to Visit

Shimla

The state capital is famous for its colonial architecture, Mall Road, Ridge and scenic Himalayan views. It was once the summer capital of British India.

Manali

A popular mountain destination in the Kullu Valley, known for its beautiful surroundings, river landscapes and access to nearby Himalayan attractions.

Dharamshala and McLeod Ganj

These mountain destinations are known for Tibetan cultural influences, monasteries, mountain views and peaceful walking routes.

Spiti Valley

A high-altitude cold desert region featuring dramatic mountains, ancient monasteries and traditional villages such as Kaza and Kibber.

Dalhousie

A charming hill station known for its colonial-era buildings, pine forests and panoramic views of the surrounding mountains.

Khajjiar

Often called the Mini Switzerland of India, Khajjiar is famous for its green meadow, forest surroundings and beautiful mountain scenery.

Great Himalayan National Park

A UNESCO World Heritage Site in the Kullu region, recognised for its rich biodiversity, mountain ecosystems and wildlife.

Chitkul

A picturesque village in the Baspa Valley of Kinnaur, known for traditional houses, mountain views and its peaceful Himalayan setting.

Traditional Himachali Food

Dham

A traditional festive meal served at community gatherings, often featuring rice, lentils, yoghurt-based dishes and regional preparations.

Siddu

A popular steamed bread made from wheat flour and often filled with a savoury mixture of local ingredients.

Madra

A traditional Chamba and Himachali dish prepared with chickpeas or other legumes in a rich yoghurt-based gravy.

Babru

A regional snack made from wheat dough filled with seasoned black gram paste and cooked until crisp.

Chha Gosht

A traditional regional curry known for its yoghurt-based gravy and distinctive blend of spices.

Aktori

A traditional festive preparation associated with Himachal's mountain regions, made using buckwheat flour.

Festivals and Celebrations

Kullu Dussehra

A famous week-long celebration in Kullu featuring processions, traditional music, cultural gatherings and decorated local deities.

Minjar Fair

Celebrated in Chamba, this traditional fair brings together folk performances, processions and community festivities.

Losar

The Tibetan New Year is celebrated in parts of Himachal with traditional ceremonies, music and cultural events.

Shivratri Fair

Mandi's International Shivratri Festival is known for its religious processions, traditional performances and colourful gatherings.

Art, Handicrafts and Cultural Heritage

Himachal Pradesh has a rich tradition of handmade crafts. Kullu shawls, Chamba rumals, Kangra paintings, wooden carvings and traditional metalwork are important parts of its artistic heritage.

Different valleys have their own dialects, clothing styles, architecture, music and customs. Traditional folk dances such as Nati remain an important part of community celebrations.

Best Time to Explore Himachal Pradesh

March to June is generally popular for pleasant weather in many lower and middle mountain destinations. December to February offers winter scenery in suitable locations. Monsoon conditions can bring heavy rain, landslides and travel disruptions, so check local weather and road advisories before travelling.
