WELCOME TO JHARKHAND

The Land of Forests and Waterfalls

Jharkhand is an eastern Indian state formed on 15 November 2000. Its name means "land of forests", reflecting the importance of its wooded landscapes and natural heritage.

The state is home to several Adivasi communities, each with distinctive languages, art forms, customs and festivals. Its hills, rivers, waterfalls and archaeological sites make Jharkhand an important part of India's natural and cultural diversity.

Famous Places to Visit

Hundru Falls

Located near Ranchi, Hundru Falls is formed by the Subarnarekha River. Its dramatic drop and rocky surroundings make it one of the region's best-known waterfalls.

Dassam Falls

A beautiful waterfall near Taimara village, where the Kanchi River descends through a rocky landscape surrounded by greenery.

Netarhat

Often called the Queen of Chotanagpur, Netarhat is known for its hills, forests, pleasant surroundings and scenic sunrise and sunset viewpoints.

Betla National Park

Situated in the Palamu region, this protected area is known for its forests, wildlife and rich natural environment.

Deoghar

An important pilgrimage destination, Deoghar is famous for Baba Baidyanath Dham, one of the twelve Jyotirlingas.

Parasnath Hills

A significant Jain pilgrimage site in Giridih district, known for its sacred temples, forested slopes and mountain landscape.

Jonha Falls

Also known as Gautamdhara, this waterfall near Ranchi is surrounded by natural scenery and is a popular local sightseeing destination.

Maluti Temples

Located in Dumka district, this historic temple village is known for its terracotta temple architecture and intricate decorative details.

Traditional Jharkhand Food

Dhuska

A popular savoury snack made from a rice and lentil batter, usually served with potato curry or chutney.

Rugra

A seasonal forest mushroom used in regional cooking and valued in several local food traditions.

Chilka Roti

A traditional pancake-like preparation made with rice and lentil batter, commonly enjoyed with chutney.

Pitha

A family of traditional rice-based preparations that can be steamed or cooked with different sweet and savoury fillings.

Handia-style Rice Traditions

Rice has an important place in local food culture. Traditional rice preparations vary across communities and regions.

Arsa Roti

A traditional sweet made with rice flour and jaggery, prepared for special occasions in parts of the region.

Festivals and Celebrations

Sarhul

A major spring festival among several Adivasi communities, celebrating nature, new beginnings and the sal tree.

Karma Festival

Celebrated in many communities with traditional songs, dances and rituals connected with the Karam tree.

Sohrai

A harvest and cattle-related festival known for beautiful wall paintings and traditional community celebrations.

Tusu Parab

A seasonal folk festival celebrated in parts of the Chotanagpur region with songs and colourful traditions.

Tribal Art and Cultural Heritage

Jharkhand has a rich cultural heritage shaped by communities such as the Santhal, Munda, Oraon and Ho. Their traditions include distinctive music, dances, oral storytelling, festivals and craft practices.

Sohrai and Khovar paintings are notable examples of regional wall art. Traditional instruments, folk dances and handmade crafts continue to play an important role in community life.

Best Time to Explore Jharkhand

October to March is generally suitable for exploring many destinations in Jharkhand. Waterfalls and forests are especially attractive during and after the monsoon, but heavy rain can affect access and safety. Always follow local travel and weather advisories.
