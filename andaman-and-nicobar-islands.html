WELCOME TO THE ISLANDS

About Andaman and Nicobar Islands

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are a group of islands located in the Bay of Bengal. This Union Territory is known for its beautiful beaches, coral reefs, tropical forests and diverse marine life. Its capital is Sri Vijaya Puram, formerly known as Port Blair.

The territory consists of two main island groups: the Andaman Islands in the north and the Nicobar Islands in the south. These islands are separated by the Ten Degree Channel.

HISTORY

A Journey Through History

The islands have a long history connected with indigenous communities and maritime routes across the Bay of Bengal. During British rule, the British established a penal settlement here. The Cellular Jail in Port Blair became an important place in India's freedom struggle.

Many Indian freedom fighters were imprisoned in the Cellular Jail. The islands were also associated with the Indian independence movement during the Second World War. In 1956, the Andaman and Nicobar Islands became a Union Territory of India.

PLACES TO EXPLORE

Famous Tourist Destinations

Cellular Jail

A historic prison and national memorial that remembers the sacrifices of Indian freedom fighters.

Swaraj Dweep

Formerly known as Havelock Island, it is famous for scenic beaches and clear waters.

Shaheed Dweep

Formerly known as Neil Island, it is known for peaceful beaches and natural coastal scenery.

Baratang Island

Known for its mangrove creeks, limestone caves and natural landscapes.

Ross Island

Now known as Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose Island, it contains historic remains from the colonial period.

North Bay Island

A popular destination for viewing marine scenery and learning about the region's coral reef ecosystem.

CULTURE & PEOPLE

Culture and Traditions

The islands have a diverse population with communities from different parts of India. Bengali, Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Malayalam and other languages are spoken across the territory.

Indigenous communities, including the Great Andamanese, Onge, Jarawa, Sentinelese and Nicobarese, have their own distinct histories and cultures. Their privacy, land and way of life deserve respect and protection.

NATURE & WILDLIFE

A Rich Natural World

The Andaman and Nicobar Islands are home to tropical rainforests, mangroves, coral reefs and many species of birds, fish and marine animals. The region is especially important for biodiversity and conservation.

Protected areas such as Mahatma Gandhi Marine National Park help conserve the islands' marine ecosystems and wildlife.

FOOD & FLAVOURS

Local Food

Food in the islands reflects influences from several Indian regions. Seafood, rice, coconut-based dishes and fresh tropical fruits are common parts of the local food culture.
