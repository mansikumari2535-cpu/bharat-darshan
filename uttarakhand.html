Welcome to Uttarakhand

Uttarakhand is a Himalayan state in northern India, celebrated for its mountain scenery, spiritual heritage, forests and rivers. It is often called Devbhoomi, meaning Land of the Gods, because of its many important pilgrimage destinations.

Dehradun is the winter capital, while Gairsain is the summer capital. The state is divided into two main regions: Garhwal and Kumaon.

History of Uttarakhand

The history of Uttarakhand is closely connected with ancient Himalayan communities, religious traditions and regional kingdoms. The Katyuri and Chand dynasties played important roles in the history of the Kumaon region, while Garhwal developed under its own line of rulers.

The region later became part of British India and was included in the United Provinces after independence. Uttarakhand was formed as a separate state on 9 November 2000, originally under the name Uttaranchal. It was renamed Uttarakhand in 2007.

Culture and Traditions

Uttarakhand has a rich cultural identity shaped by Garhwali and Kumaoni traditions. Folk music, traditional dances, local festivals, woodcraft and handwoven textiles are important parts of its heritage.

Traditional celebrations include Harela, Phool Dei, Nanda Devi Raj Jat and various regional fairs. The state's culture is closely connected with its mountain environment and community life.

Famous Food of Uttarakhand

Kafuli

A traditional green-leaf curry associated with Uttarakhand cuisine.

Chainsoo

A nutritious regional dish prepared using roasted black gram.

Mandua Roti

A traditional flatbread made with finger millet flour.

Bal Mithai

A famous Kumaoni sweet made from roasted khoya and coated with sugar balls.

Festivals of Uttarakhand

Kumbh Mela in Haridwar is one of India's major religious gatherings. Other important festivals include Harela, Nanda Devi celebrations, Phool Dei and the traditional fairs of Garhwal and Kumaon.

Wildlife and Natural Heritage

Uttarakhand contains diverse Himalayan ecosystems, from river valleys and forests to alpine meadows and high mountain habitats. Jim Corbett National Park is one of India's best-known tiger reserves.

Nanda Devi National Park and the Valley of Flowers National Park form part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site. The state also supports elephants, leopards, musk deer and many Himalayan bird species.
