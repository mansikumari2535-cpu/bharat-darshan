Welcome to Sikkim

Sikkim is a small Himalayan state in northeastern India, known for its dramatic mountain landscapes, rich biodiversity and cultural diversity. It shares borders with Nepal, Bhutan and the Tibet Autonomous Region of China, as well as West Bengal.

Gangtok is the state capital. Sikkim is home to Kanchenjunga, the world's third-highest mountain, and is famous for its monasteries, alpine lakes and beautiful valleys.

History of Sikkim

Sikkim was historically ruled by the Namgyal dynasty, which established the Kingdom of Sikkim in the seventeenth century. The region developed distinctive traditions influenced by Himalayan communities and Tibetan Buddhism.

Sikkim became India's twenty-second state on 16 May 1975. Its history reflects a unique journey from a Himalayan kingdom to a state within the Indian Union.

Famous Tourist Destinations

Gangtok

The capital city, known for mountain views, monasteries and lively markets.

Tsomgo Lake

A beautiful high-altitude glacial lake surrounded by Himalayan scenery.

Nathula Pass

A historic mountain pass on the India-China border region.

Pelling

A scenic destination known for views of Kanchenjunga and nearby heritage sites.

Yumthang Valley

Often called the Valley of Flowers, known for alpine landscapes and seasonal blooms.

Rumtek Monastery

An important Buddhist monastery near Gangtok, known for its architecture and traditions.

Culture and Traditions

Sikkim has a diverse cultural heritage shaped by Lepcha, Bhutia and Nepali communities. Buddhism and Hinduism have both influenced the region's festivals, architecture, music and everyday traditions.

Traditional celebrations include Losar, Losoong, Saga Dawa and Pang Lhabsol. Monasteries, prayer flags, traditional dances and community gatherings are important parts of Sikkimese culture.

Famous Food of Sikkim

Momos

Steamed dumplings filled with vegetables or other fillings, commonly served with chutney.

Thukpa

A warming noodle soup popular across Himalayan communities.

Phagshapa

A traditional Sikkimese dish prepared with radish and meat.

Gundruk

A fermented leafy-green preparation used in traditional Himalayan meals.

Wildlife and Natural Heritage

Sikkim is known for its exceptional biodiversity, ranging from subtropical forests to alpine meadows and high mountain habitats. Khangchendzonga National Park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site recognised for its natural and cultural importance.

The state is home to red pandas, Himalayan black bears, snow leopards and many bird species. Its forests also contain a rich variety of orchids, rhododendrons and other mountain plants.
