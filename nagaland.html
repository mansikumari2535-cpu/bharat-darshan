Nagaland

Discover the land of colourful Naga traditions, mountain villages, lively festivals and beautiful green landscapes.

Welcome to Nagaland

Nagaland is a mountainous state in Northeast India, known for its rich cultural diversity and scenic hills. Its capital is Kohima, while Dimapur is an important commercial centre and transport gateway.

Many Naga communities live across the state, each with distinctive languages, customs, crafts and traditions. English is the official language, and Nagamese is widely used for communication in many areas.

History and Heritage

Nagaland's history is closely connected with its many Naga communities and their traditional village institutions. Each community developed its own customs, oral histories, festivals and forms of craftsmanship.

The Naga Hills became part of independent India, and Nagaland attained full statehood on 1 December 1963. The state's heritage continues to be preserved through community celebrations, traditional knowledge and cultural institutions.

Culture and Traditions

Naga culture is known for its colourful shawls, beadwork, wood carving, folk songs and traditional dances. Patterns and designs often reflect the identity and heritage of particular communities.

The Hornbill Festival, held annually in December near Kisama Heritage Village, brings together performances, crafts, food and cultural displays from different Naga communities. Sekrenyi, Moatsu and Aoling are among the important community festivals.

Traditional Cuisine

Naga cuisine features rice, vegetables, herbs, bamboo shoots and locally grown ingredients. Smoked foods and fermented preparations are part of several regional food traditions.

Popular dishes include smoked pork with bamboo shoot, galho, axone-based preparations and a variety of vegetable dishes. Recipes differ among communities and households.

Famous Tourist Places

Kohima

The capital city, known for its cultural heritage and historic landmarks.

Khonoma

A historic village recognised for community-led forest conservation.

Dzükou Valley

A scenic valley famous for rolling hills and seasonal flowers.

Mon

A district known for the traditions and heritage of the Konyak community.

Dimapur

An important city with historic ruins and modern markets.

Mokokchung

A hill town associated with Ao Naga culture and traditions.

Wildlife and Nature

Nagaland's forests and mountain landscapes support a wide variety of plants, birds and animals. Intanki National Park, also known as Ntangki National Park, is an important protected area in the state.

The state is also known for its birdlife, including the Amur falcon, which passes through the region during seasonal migration. Community conservation initiatives play an important role in protecting local habitats.

Discover More of Nagaland

From traditional Naga villages and colourful festivals to mountain valleys and rich biodiversity, Nagaland offers a distinctive view of Northeast India's heritage.
