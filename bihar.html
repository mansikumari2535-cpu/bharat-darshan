Bihar

The Ancient Land of Knowledge, Spirituality, Learning and Living Traditions.

Discover Bihar

Where Ancient History Comes Alive

Bihar is an eastern Indian state with a remarkable history connected to ancient kingdoms, universities, Buddhism, Jainism and the development of Indian philosophy. The River Ganga flows through the state, shaping its agriculture, settlements and cultural life.

Explore the state

Famous Places

Explore the monuments, pilgrimage destinations and historic learning centres of Bihar.

Bodh Gaya

An internationally important Buddhist pilgrimage destination associated with the enlightenment of Gautama Buddha.

Nalanda

Home to the remains of the ancient Nalanda Mahavihara, a renowned centre of learning and a UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Patna

Bihar's capital, historically known as Pataliputra, with museums, monuments and a rich connection to ancient Indian history.

Rajgir

An ancient historic town surrounded by hills and associated with Buddhist and Jain traditions.

Vaishali

An important archaeological and religious site associated with ancient republics and the lives of Buddha and Mahavira.

Vikramshila

The remains of an important ancient Buddhist learning centre established during the Pala period.

Taste of tradition

Traditional Food

Bihari cuisine features wheat, rice, lentils, seasonal vegetables and distinctive regional preparations.

Litti Chokha

A well-known Bihari dish featuring roasted wheat balls filled with sattu, served with mashed vegetables.

Dal Pitha

Rice-flour dumplings filled with seasoned lentils, enjoyed in different parts of Bihar.

Thekua

A traditional sweet snack made with wheat flour and jaggery, especially associated with Chhath Puja.

Khaja

A layered, crisp traditional sweet particularly associated with Silao near Rajgir.

Malpua

A traditional sweet pancake prepared for festivals and special occasions.

Sattu Paratha

A popular stuffed flatbread made with roasted gram flour and aromatic seasonings.

Art and celebrations

Festivals & Culture

Bihar's cultural traditions include folk music, painting, handcrafts, religious celebrations and seasonal festivals.

Chhath Puja

A major festival dedicated to the Sun, celebrated with prayers, traditional offerings and community gatherings near water bodies.

Sama-Chakeva

A traditional festival celebrated in the Mithila region, associated with folk songs and handmade clay figures.

Madhubani Painting

A celebrated Mithila art tradition known for intricate patterns, natural themes and colourful compositions.

Jat-Jatin

A traditional folk dance of Bihar that tells stories of everyday life and relationships through music and movement.

Sonepur Mela

A historic fair held near the confluence of the Ganga and Gandak rivers, known for its cultural and religious significance.

Jitiya

A traditional observance in Bihar and neighbouring regions, marked by family customs and community traditions.
