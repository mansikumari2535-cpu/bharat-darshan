Punjab

Discover the land of five rivers, rich history, vibrant folk traditions, warm hospitality and celebrated cuisine.

Welcome to Punjab

Punjab is a state in northwestern India, known for its fertile plains, agricultural heritage and lively cultural traditions. Its capital is Chandigarh, which it shares with Haryana. Punjabi is the official language of the state.

Punjab has a distinctive identity shaped by its history, music, literature, farming communities and religious heritage. Amritsar, Ludhiana, Jalandhar and Patiala are among its important cities.

History and Heritage

The Punjab region has been an important centre of civilisation, trade and cultural exchange for centuries. Its history includes ancient settlements, medieval kingdoms and events that shaped modern South Asia.

Sikhism emerged in the Punjab region in the fifteenth century through the teachings of Guru Nanak Dev Ji and the Sikh Gurus. The region also played a significant role in India's freedom movement. The present state of Punjab was formed on 1 November 1966 after the reorganisation of the former Punjab state.

Culture and Traditions

Punjab is known for its energetic folk dances, music, colourful clothing and community celebrations. Bhangra and Giddha are among its best-known traditional dance forms.

Vaisakhi is an important festival associated with the harvest season and Sikh history. Lohri, Gurpurab and other regional celebrations bring communities together through music, food and cultural activities.

Phulkari embroidery is a recognised traditional textile art of Punjab, known for its colourful floral patterns.

Traditional Cuisine

Punjabi cuisine is known for its hearty meals, breads, lentils, dairy-based dishes and rich regional flavours. Popular foods include makki di roti with sarson da saag, chole bhature, rajma chawal and parathas.

Lassi, kadhi pakora and a variety of traditional sweets are also enjoyed across the region. Recipes vary between households and local areas.

Famous Tourist Places

Golden Temple

The famous Sikh shrine in Amritsar, known for its architecture and community kitchen.

Jallianwala Bagh

A memorial site remembering the tragic events of 1919.

Wagah Border

Known for the daily ceremonial flag-lowering event near Amritsar.

Patiala

A historic city known for its royal heritage, palaces and traditional crafts.

Anandpur Sahib

An important Sikh heritage town associated with the history of the Khalsa.

Kapurthala

A city known for its distinctive palaces and architectural landmarks.

Wildlife and Nature

Punjab's landscape includes fertile agricultural plains, rivers, wetlands and grasslands. Harike Wetland is an important habitat for migratory and resident birds.

Abohar Wildlife Sanctuary and several other protected areas help conserve regional wildlife. The state's wetlands are especially valuable for biodiversity and seasonal bird migration.

Discover More of Punjab

From historic Sikh heritage sites and traditional villages to lively festivals and famous food, Punjab offers a rich experience of India's cultural diversity.
