Maharashtra

Discover a land of historic forts, vibrant cities, ancient caves, coastal beauty and rich cultural traditions.

Welcome to Maharashtra

Maharashtra is a state in western India, with a long Arabian Sea coastline and a varied landscape of mountains, plateaus and coastal regions. Mumbai is its capital and one of India's major financial and cultural centres. Marathi is the official language.

The state is known for its historic legacy, industries, literature, performing arts, agriculture and diverse communities. Pune, Nagpur, Nashik and Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar are among its important cities.

History and Heritage

Maharashtra has a rich history shaped by ancient settlements, the Satavahana dynasty, the Deccan Sultanates and the Maratha Empire. Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj established an important Maratha kingdom in the seventeenth century, leaving a lasting legacy in the region.

The Ajanta and Ellora caves are internationally recognised for their rock-cut architecture, paintings and sculptures. Maharashtra became a state on 1 May 1960 after the division of the former Bombay State.

Culture and Traditions

Maharashtra's cultural life includes folk performances, classical music, theatre, literature and traditional crafts. Lavani and Tamasha are well-known performance traditions, while Powada is a form of ballad singing associated with historical storytelling.

Ganesh Chaturthi is one of the state's major festivals, celebrated with community gatherings, artistic decorations and cultural programmes. Gudi Padwa, Diwali and Makar Sankranti are also widely observed.

Traditional Cuisine

Maharashtrian cuisine offers a wide range of flavours, from coastal seafood dishes to hearty meals from the Deccan plateau. Popular foods include vada pav, misal pav, puran poli, thalipeeth and zunka bhakri.

Regional cooking often uses ingredients such as coconut, peanuts, lentils, millets and local spices. Food traditions vary considerably between the Konkan coast, Vidarbha, Marathwada and western Maharashtra.

Famous Tourist Places

Mumbai

A lively coastal metropolis known for the Gateway of India and historic landmarks.

Pune

A historic city known for its educational institutions, forts and cultural life.

Ajanta Caves

Ancient Buddhist caves celebrated for their paintings and sculptures.

Ellora Caves

A remarkable complex of Buddhist, Hindu and Jain rock-cut monuments.

Mahabaleshwar

A hill destination known for scenic viewpoints and strawberry farms.

Raigad Fort

A historic hill fort closely associated with Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj.

Wildlife and Nature

Maharashtra has diverse natural habitats, including forests, wetlands, grasslands and coastal ecosystems. Tadoba-Andhari Tiger Reserve, Sanjay Gandhi National Park and Navegaon National Park are among its notable protected areas.

The Western Ghats, a UNESCO World Heritage region, support rich biodiversity and seasonal waterfalls. The Konkan coastline also offers beaches, mangroves and important marine habitats.

Discover More of Maharashtra

From historic Maratha forts and ancient caves to bustling cities and peaceful coastal landscapes, Maharashtra offers many ways to explore India's heritage.
