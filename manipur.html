Manipur

Discover the jewel of Northeast India, known for its classical dance, scenic landscapes, rich traditions and vibrant heritage.

Welcome to Manipur

Manipur is a state in Northeast India, surrounded by hills and valleys. Its capital is Imphal. The state shares an international border with Myanmar and is known for its distinctive cultural traditions and natural beauty.

Meitei (Manipuri) is the official language, and several other languages are spoken by the state's diverse communities. Manipur has a long history of traditional arts, sports, agriculture and trade.

History and Heritage

Manipur has a documented history of its own kingdoms and royal traditions. The Ningthouja dynasty played an important role in the development of the historic kingdom of Manipur.

The region became part of India in 1949 and attained full statehood on 21 January 1972. Its heritage is reflected in historic sites, traditional institutions, literature and performing arts.

Culture and Traditions

Manipur is internationally recognised for Manipuri classical dance, known for its graceful movements and devotional themes. The state also has rich traditions of handloom weaving, handicrafts, music and theatre.

Lai Haraoba is an important traditional festival, while Yaoshang is widely celebrated with community activities and cultural events. Traditional sports such as Sagol Kangjei, associated with the origins of modern polo, are also part of the state's heritage.

Traditional Cuisine

Manipuri cuisine often features rice, seasonal vegetables, herbs, fish and fermented ingredients. Eromba is a well-known dish made with vegetables and fermented fish in many traditional preparations.

Other popular foods include singju, chamthong (also called kangshoi) and various local rice dishes. Recipes and ingredients differ across communities and regions.

Famous Tourist Places

Loktak Lake

Famous for its floating phumdis and distinctive wetland landscape.

Imphal

The capital city, known for its historic sites and lively markets.

Kangla Fort

A historic complex associated with the former rulers of Manipur.

Moirang

A historic town connected with the freedom movement and local traditions.

Keibul Lamjao

A unique floating national park on Loktak Lake.

Ukhrul

A hill destination known for scenic views and Shirui lilies.

Wildlife and Nature

Manipur has a rich natural environment of hills, forests, rivers and wetlands. Keibul Lamjao National Park is particularly important because it provides habitat for the endangered Sangai, also known as the dancing deer.

Loktak Lake is an important freshwater ecosystem and supports local livelihoods and wildlife. The state's forests and hills are home to many bird and animal species.
