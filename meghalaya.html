Meghalaya

Discover the abode of clouds, where green hills, dramatic waterfalls, living root bridges and vibrant traditions create unforgettable landscapes.

Welcome to Meghalaya

Meghalaya is a beautiful state in Northeast India, known for its hills, forests, caves and exceptionally scenic landscapes. Its name means "abode of clouds". Shillong is the state capital and an important centre of education, music and culture.

Khasi, Garo and Jaintia communities have shaped much of the state's cultural identity. Khasi and Garo are among the major languages spoken, alongside English, which is the official language of the state.

History and Heritage

The history of Meghalaya is closely connected with the traditional kingdoms and communities of the Khasi, Jaintia and Garo Hills. These communities developed distinctive systems of governance, customary laws, crafts and social traditions.

Meghalaya was part of Assam before becoming an autonomous state in 1970. It attained full statehood on 21 January 1972.

Culture and Traditions

Meghalaya has a rich cultural life expressed through traditional music, dance, weaving, festivals and community celebrations. The Wangala festival of the Garo people is a harvest celebration featuring music and traditional drumming.

Shad Suk Mynsiem and Behdienkhlam are important festivals associated with Khasi and Jaintia traditions. Many communities in Meghalaya follow matrilineal customs, in which family descent and inheritance are traditionally traced through the mother's line.

Traditional Cuisine

Meghalayan cuisine makes use of rice, seasonal vegetables, local herbs, bamboo shoots and regional ingredients. Jadoh, a rice dish commonly prepared with meat, is associated with Khasi cuisine.

Other traditional foods include dohneiiong, tungrymbai and nakham bitchi. Food traditions vary among the Khasi, Garo and Jaintia communities.

Famous Tourist Places

Shillong

The capital city, known for its hills, music scene and colonial-era architecture.

Cherrapunji

Famous for heavy rainfall, waterfalls, caves and dramatic valleys.

Mawlynnong

A village known for community-led cleanliness and nearby living root bridges.

Dawki

Known for the clear waters of the Umngot River and scenic surroundings.

Jowai

A hill town surrounded by green landscapes and cultural heritage sites.

Mawsmai Cave

A limestone cave known for its natural rock formations.

Wildlife and Nature

Meghalaya's natural environment includes subtropical forests, limestone caves, rivers and wetlands. Nokrek Biosphere Reserve and Balpakram National Park are among its important protected areas.

The state is also famous for its living root bridges, created by guiding the roots of rubber trees across streams. These remarkable structures reflect generations of local knowledge and a close relationship with nature.

Discover More of Meghalaya

From misty hills and spectacular waterfalls to living root bridges and distinctive community traditions, Meghalaya offers a memorable glimpse of Northeast India's heritage.
